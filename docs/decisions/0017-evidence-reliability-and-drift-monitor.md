# ADR 0017 — Evidence reliability: eligibility-checked priors and a windowed drift monitor (M6.2)

Status: Accepted as **implemented, gated and opt-in (monitor mode defaults to `SHADOW`)**. The pre-registered hypothesis is **partially supported**: detection, scoping, small-sample conservatism, SHADOW non-interference and outcome improvement over M6.1's guard hold on frozen synthetic scenarios; recovery after a temporary disturbance does not meet its target (40% vs 70%). The rule baseline stays the default controller and nothing was promoted. Evidence: [design](../experiments/evidence-reliability-design.md), [report](../evaluations/evidence-reliability-report.md), [tables](../evaluations/evidence-reliability-tables.md), [calibration](../evaluations/evidence-reliability-calibration.md); operating guide [controller.md](../controller.md). Builds on ADR [0016](./0016-evidence-driven-controller.md).

## Context
M6.1 showed that paired validation evidence can replace exploration, but (i) the evidence came from a separate paired run whose provenance and eligibility nothing checked, the promotion evaluator's capability-alone arm could not produce it, and (ii) a prior that goes stale was handled by a cumulative guard that is slow, stateless, unaudited and cannot recover. M6.2 asks whether experience can be given a reliability lifecycle: acquire it through the real workflow, assess it before use, detect when it stops being trustworthy, stop using it safely, and recover only on fresh evidence.

## Decisions
1. **Final-outcome evidence is recorded by the evaluation workflow, beside the verdict, never inside it.** `Arm.SERVING` (the capability as production serves it, planner fallback included, evaluation-only static catalog) runs after and independently of the promotion decision; `decide()` never reads it and every earlier verdict, suite and config hash is unchanged without it. The paired table is stored in the append-only evaluation record (`metrics.serving_evidence`).
2. **Eligibility is an explicit gate list, not a score.** `EvidenceAssessment` lists one reason per failed gate (kind, tenant, risk class, held-out partitions, runtime/verifier/environment versions, capability version, sample size, UNKNOWN/tie fraction, token measurements, duplicates, evaluation verdict, test-double model, freshness, drift state). Ineligible evidence cannot train a production prior (`--allow-research` records it as research-only); the existing version override is kept, audited in the source and weakens no other gate. Result files are research-only.
3. **The monitor answers one question** — are recent verified outcomes inconsistent enough with the evidence behind the decision to stop trusting it — and never "what is better". It folds executed, verified outcomes of a cell (shadow decisions, UNKNOWN and other tenants excluded) through `NORMAL → SUSPECT → DRIFTED → QUARANTINED → RECOVERING → NORMAL`, with channels for success, cost, utility, context mismatch and staleness, a persistence rule, and a stricter, separately thresholded recovery. A quarantined prior cannot control decisions, including through the unpaired live rule.
4. **Windowed comparison over CUSUM.** Both were implemented and calibrated on development seeds; CUSUM had no feasible point (it reacts to block-level noise). By the pre-registered rule the simpler detector is the candidate. Held-out comparison (reported, not used to choose): CUSUM has more false alarms, is slower on abrupt and faster on gradual drift.
5. **Recovery is explicit, bounded and conservative.** A quarantine period of rule-served decisions, then probes (probe mass, per-episode budget, existing exploration allow-list, exact logged propensities), then `RECOVERY_CRITERIA_MET` on ≥ 30 fresh outcomes with a lower bound above a stricter threshold than the one that triggered drift, acceptable cost and utility, compatible context, for several consecutive looks; an episode cap leaves a repeatedly failing cell quarantined until a new version. The historical prior is never edited.
6. **Shadow first, enforcement as a new version.** `DriftParams.mode` is part of the immutable policy definition: `SHADOW` records transitions with `applied=false` and leaves every decision bit-identical to M6.1 (tested on every held-out stream); `ENFORCE` is a different definition hash and therefore needs its own offline → shadow → canary evidence. The monitor can only restrict a policy toward the rule baseline; protected classes, foreign tenants and non-allow-listed families never read it. The overlay is published to the controller when it is built, never per decision.
7. **Transitions are persisted, replayable and verified.** Migration 0007 adds the append-only `controller_drift_transitions` table (full threshold configuration, its hash, measurements, detector version, causing decision). `DriftService.sync` refuses to append unless the persisted log is exactly a prefix of the recomputation; decisions that read an overlay replay exactly and tampering with the stored overlay is detected.
8. **Backward compatibility is part of the contract.** All new fields are optional and omitted from serialisation when unset; the M6 and M6.1 definition and snapshot hashes and the decision digests of M6, M6.1 evidence-driven, bandit and cold policies are pinned (the digests were computed with the M6.1 code before any M6.2 change).

## Findings (details and caveats in the report)
* **Detection (held-out, synthetic, 300 streams):** abrupt success collapse median 47 decisions, 0/300 missed; cost-only 78, 3.3% missed; gradual 207, 2.7% missed; runtime change 13 and staleness 10 (structural, no sample needed); context-specific drift isolated (`pool` 0/300 false alarms).
* **False alarms:** 0/300 stable, 2.3% noisy (exact 95% upper bound 4.3%); none below the minimum sample; a *severe* 25-observation transient still triggers DRIFTED in 20.7% of streams (informational scenario).
* **Outcomes vs M6.1 (100 streams per scenario):** +20 verified successes per 800 decisions after an abrupt collapse [+16, +24], +27 after gradual degradation, utility positive in every persistent-drift scenario; SHADOW identical to M6.1 on every stream.
* **Negative:** recovery. 40/100 temporary-disturbance streams returned to NORMAL (target 70%; median 284 decisions after the disturbance ended). The frozen probe mass 0.25 gives an effective 12.5% probe probability (the exploration draw includes the greedy action; found after the held-out run). Exploratory development-seed sensitivity: probe mass 0.5 reaches 63% at +62% probe tokens and halves the benefit under persistent drift: recovery speed is a cost trade, not a free fix.
* **Negative:** structural invalidation with unchanged outcomes (runtime/age) costs +22–25% tokens for −2% successes against M6.1 (S8/S8b); nothing refreshes the evidence automatically.
* **Real-model track:** a replay defect of mine (version labels) produced 20/20 false alarms through the context-mismatch channel, which was correct; after the fix 0/20 in stable real-outcome streams (too few to bound the rate below 13.9%).
* **Calibration lesson:** the noisy scenario, not the abrupt one, sets the confidence level; aggressive settings raise false alarms 8–45× on development data.

## Consequences
* Operators can train a policy from stored evaluations (`ee-eval evaluate --serving-evidence`, `ee-ctl train --paired-from-registry … --drift-monitor shadow`), inspect eligibility (`ee-ctl evidence-inspect`), run and audit the monitor (`drift-evaluate|status|replay|shadow-report`) and reproduce the experiments (`drift-calibrate|sim|real|report`). Adoption of an `ENFORCE` version still needs offline, shadow and canary evidence under the unchanged M6 gates and a fresh confirmation; M6.1's own prior is research-only under these rules.
* Next (M6.3): an adaptive probe schedule evaluated on fresh seeds, automatic revalidation of structurally invalidated evidence, one real-model serving-evidence evaluation and a live shadow run of an M6.2 policy, then a canary of an ENFORCE version.
* Not done: latency monitoring, variant-level learning, automatic capability retirement or regeneration, multi-tenant pooled monitors, an alpha-spending treatment of the sequential looks, real-world drift evidence.
