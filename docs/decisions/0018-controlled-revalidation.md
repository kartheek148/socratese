# ADR 0018 — Controlled revalidation of quarantined cells (M6.3)

Status: Accepted as **implemented, gated and opt-in** (a policy version must carry `params.revalidation`; `SHADOW` records readiness and executes nothing). The pre-registered hypothesis is **partially supported**: the protocol is safe, restores more, faster and without exposing users, and uniquely recovers structurally invalidated and stale evidence — but it is not economical in the benchmark world as frozen, and a verification outage is mishandled. The rule baseline stays the default controller, M6.2's native recovery stays available and unchanged, and nothing was promoted. Evidence: [audit](../research/m6_3_revalidation_audit.md), [frozen design](../experiments/revalidation-design.md), [report](../evaluations/revalidation-report.md), [tables](../evaluations/revalidation-tables.md); operating guide [controller.md](../controller.md). Builds on ADR [0017](./0017-evidence-reliability-and-drift-monitor.md).

## Context
M6.2 quarantined a cell whose evidence stopped being trustworthy and let it back through a *timer* followed by live probes. The M6.3 audit (before any code) found that this path cannot recover structural invalidation or stale evidence at all (35 attempts, 0 recoveries on a runtime change; 0 attempts on staleness), accepts a *fully recovered* world in only 34–41 % of looks, sends ~100 production probes per episode to the quarantined alternative, and records neither the reason, the lineage nor the cost of recovery. The question: can a quarantined cell be restored through bounded, independently verified, out-of-band revalidation, reason by reason, so that every restoration is explainable from stored evidence — and at what lifecycle cost?

## Decisions
1. **Layer, don't rewrite.** The M6.2 state machine, `DriftParams`, the overlay and every stored M6/M6.1/M6.2 hash are unchanged. A *revalidation case* is a second machine per quarantine episode, composed with the M6.2 fleet; it disables native recovery through the existing `locked` flag. Two additive `MonitorFleet` helpers (`restore`, `replace_reference`) are the only edits to M6.2 code. `ControllerParams.revalidation` is omitted when unset; revalidation changes no decision until a cell is quarantined (tested).
2. **Reasons are a projection of the existing drift triggers**, not a parallel enum of detectors. `UNKNOWN` is ineligible; `VERIFICATION_FAILURE` exists for externally supplied quarantines only.
3. **Recovery is never automatic.** Eligibility is a gate list (reason, tenant, risk class, identity, versions, verification available, minimum pool, lifetime budget, cycles, TTL, readiness); structural and statistical cases follow different protocols (a version change is never evidence of recovery: the capability is re-tested under the *current* version and only then is the reference rebased, as a logged event).
4. **States** `QUARANTINED → REVALIDATION_PENDING → REVALIDATING → RECOVERY_CANDIDATE → RECOVERED` with terminal `EXPIRED` and `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. Every quarantined cell is prior-disabled in every state except `RECOVERED`, with no live probes. A genuine distinction from M6.2's `RECOVERING`: evidence comes from bounded, verified, out-of-band trials, not from user traffic.
5. **One hypothesis, three strategies.** Success not worse than the reference by ≥ 0.10 with exact binomial error control and a cost guard: A (fixed test), B (A behind an evidence-readiness gate), C (SPRT). A cost-futility stop (design erratum E1) and realistic trial spacing (E2) were added before any held-out data. No Bayesian or adaptive policy.
6. **Verification is independent and conservative.** Five verdicts; only an explicit pass is a success; unavailable verdicts never count, consume budget and end the cycle when frequent. `OrchestratorTrialSource` runs trials through the production orchestrator and verifier with a forced action and **no run lifecycle**, so a trial can never become a production decision or a monitor observation.
7. **Lineage on every row; append-only persistence; exact replay.** Migration 0008 adds `controller_revalidation_transitions` and `controller_revalidation_trials` (append-only triggers; downgrade refuses with rows). `RevalidationService.sync` recomputes from the ordered outcomes and the persisted trials only, refuses to append unless the stored log is exactly what it recomputes, and publishes the overlay; `.run` is the learning-plane worker. The M6.2 `DriftService` refuses revalidation policies (a plain replay would miss the restorations).
8. **Shadow-first.** In `SHADOW` cases open and readiness is recorded with `applied=false`; no trial is executed and no decision changes (tested bit-identical to M6.1 under drift).

## Findings (details and caveats in the report)
* **Safety:** false restoration 1/400, 1/400, 0/400 (A/B/C) where the alternative is materially degraded (M6.2 native: 5/100 in R1 alone; upper bound 10.2 %).
* **Recovery:** restored by the end of the stream S5 84 % (C) vs 51 % native; R3 83 vs 44; structural S8 and stale S8b 90 vs 0. Median delay after a disturbance 80 vs 305 decisions. Zero live probes (native: 85–105 per stream).
* **Sequential testing:** C spends 45 % [43–47 %] of A's tokens at equal or better safety and restoration; B was identical to A on 938/1,000 streams and differed only in R3, where it saved 11 % of the tokens at identical restoration; not adopted on this evidence (untested combined with C).
* **Negative — economics:** pooled net utility vs M6.2 −29.7 [−33.6, −25.8] (C), −59.4 (A); vs permanent quarantine −21.1. Break-even ≈ 370 restored decisions per ≈ 1M tokens; an 800-decision stream restores around position 480–600. {{HORIZON}}
* **Negative — verification outage (R4):** a revalidation-verifier outage consumed all failed cycles (40/100 C streams ended `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`); restoration 55–59 % vs the 70 % target. Unavailable trials were never counted (0 violations), but treating an outage as a failed cycle is a design flaw.
* **Negative — relapse (R5):** restoring then relapsing exposes users to up to +50 degraded decisions per stream beyond permanent quarantine; the M6.2 monitor re-quarantines but cannot prevent it.
* **Not exercised by the experiment:** lineage rejections (0 occurred; unit-tested), the `STATISTICAL_DRIFT`, `VERIFICATION_FAILURE` and `UNKNOWN` reasons, the `confirm_trials` value (exploratory ablation only), any real revalidation pool.

## Consequences
* Operators can opt a policy version into revalidation (`params.revalidation`), run the worker (`RevalidationService.run` with an executor), inspect it (`ee-ctl reval-status | reval-evaluate | reval-replay`) and reproduce the experiment (`reval-sim | reval-report`). Adoption of an `ENFORCE` revalidation version still needs offline, shadow and canary evidence under the unchanged M6 gates, a revalidation pool of independently verified, side-effect-free tasks, and a stated horizon over which its cost is repaid.
* Next (M6.4): **reason-aware routing** (structural and stale quarantines to revalidation, statistical ones keep cheap native probe recovery with revalidation as the bounded fallback; exploratory evidence only); separate *verification unavailable* from *criteria not met*; an economic eligibility gate (expected benefit over a stated horizon vs expected trial cost); a pre-registered margin/α study including the boundary scenario R1; the missing provenance and freshness gates; a live shadow run and a real revalidation pool; then a real-model acceptance experiment and a canary. B's readiness gate is not adopted on this evidence; do not tune the frozen parameters on held-out seeds.
* Not done: eligibility gates for evidence provenance (the prior's production-eligibility is not re-checked at revalidation) and for evidence freshness as such, latency monitoring, pooled or multi-family monitors, automatic capability retirement or regeneration, an alpha-spending treatment across cycles, real-world drift evidence.
